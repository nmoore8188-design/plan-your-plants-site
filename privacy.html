Privacy Policy

Effective October 5, 2026

The short version: Plan Your Plants keeps your plants, notes, and photos on your phone. There's no account, no ads, no analytics, and no tracking. The only thing that leaves your phone is a weather lookup, and only if you set one up.

What the app keeps

Everything you put in the app: plant names, spots, care logs, soil and light readings, reminders, notes, and photos. We (the developer) don't collect any of it. It's never sent to us, and we don't run a server that receives it.

There's no account or sign-in, so the app doesn't ask for your name, email, or phone number.

Where it's stored

On your iPhone, inside the app. Records go in a small database in the app's own storage, and photos are saved in the app's own folder.

  • Camera and photos. The camera is used to snap plants, spots, and light tests. If you pick a picture from your photo library, the app copies just that picture. It doesn't browse or upload your library.
  • Reminders. Reminders are scheduled on the phone itself. They don't go through a push service, and the app doesn't register for remote notifications.
  • Home screen widget. If you add the widget, the app copies one growth photo (and that plant's name, unless you turn on Hide plant names) into a shared folder on your phone so the widget can show it. That copy stays on the phone.
  • Backups. You can export a ZIP backup of your plants, records, and photos. It's saved where you choose, using the iPhone share sheet. The app never uploads it anywhere. If you send it somewhere (like iCloud Drive or email), that service's terms apply.
  • Phone backups. If your iPhone backs up to iCloud or a computer, Apple's backup may include the app's data, under Apple's terms.

Third parties and what's sent

Weather (Open-Meteo)

The outside forecast comes from Open-Meteo, a weather service. It's optional, and indoor plants don't need it.

  • If you allow location, the app reads your position while it's open, rounds it to about a kilometer, and sends only those rounded coordinates to Open-Meteo to get a 7-day forecast.
  • If you type a city instead, that city name is sent to Open-Meteo to look it up.
  • Nothing else goes with it: no name, no plants, no photos, no notes, no device ID.
  • Like any web request, Open-Meteo can see your IP address. See Open-Meteo's terms for how they handle requests.

The app only asks for location "while using the app," never in the background.

Apple

Now and then the app may show Apple's standard "rate this app" prompt. Apple runs that prompt. The app just keeps a note on your phone of when it last asked, so it doesn't ask too often.

Outside links

A few tips link to outside pages (for example, a YouTube video). They only open if you tap them, and that site's own privacy policy applies.

Not live yet

Plant Doctor and identifying plants from a photo aren't turned on. Nothing is sent for them. If that ever changes, we'll update this policy first and say what's sent and to whom.

No selling, no tracking

We don't sell or share your data, because we don't have it. The app has no ads, no analytics, no crash reporting service, and no tracking across apps or websites.

Children

The app isn't made for children under 13, but it doesn't collect personal information from anyone, kids included.

Deleting your data

  • Delete a plant, photo, or reading right in the app.
  • To remove everything, delete the app from your iPhone. That erases its database, photos, and widget copy.
  • Any backup ZIP you exported, and any iPhone backups, are yours to delete separately.

Since we never receive your data, there's nothing for us to delete on our end.

Changes

If this policy changes, we'll post the new version here and update the date at the top. If the app ever starts sending something new off your phone, we'll say so here before it does.
